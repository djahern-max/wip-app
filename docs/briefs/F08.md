## D-39 · 2026-10-06 · A fuel surcharge is outside the contract, is recognised as billed, and prints on the pay application

**Decision**: A fuel surcharge on a fixed-price construction or excavation job is not part of the contract. It is not in the estimate, is not a work area, and never enters the revised contract, EAC, percent complete or earned revenue. The tenant's surcharge rate is a policy value decided by a person (Rye Beach: 0.0500); there is no default. On each pay application a person says whether the surcharge applies; nothing is assumed. When it applies, the surcharge is the application's amount due × the rate, quantized `ROUND_HALF_UP` to the cent, printed as its own line, "Fuel surcharge (5.00%)", below the amount due, followed by the total to invoice.

In QuickBooks the surcharge is a line on the same invoice, on one of the tenant's fuel surcharge items (one item per division income account). The platform recognises a surcharge line by its item id only, never by its description or amount. An invoice ties to its pay application (D-36) in two parts: its surcharge lines equal the printed surcharge, and its total less surcharge lines equals the amount due. A surcharge on an invoice with no pay application, including the deposit invoice (D-02), is keyed by hand on the same items and is recognised the same way.

Surcharge lines are excluded from billed to date (§8.2 column 10) on invoices and credit memos alike, and so from over / (under) billed and the WIP entry. The revenue stays in the division income account in the month it is invoiced; no entry is posted for it. The job shows it as its own figure, "Fuel surcharge billed", and the tie-out shows the total as revenue outside the schedule, as D-24 does for T&M. Collected to date and open A/R remain whole-invoice figures and include it.

When fuel is priced into an estimate, it is contract price like any other and this decision does not apply to it; a job carries a surcharge only where a pay application or an invoice line says so.

**Reasoning**: The surcharge is new, optional per customer and per invoice, and posts to the same income account as contract billing, so the account cannot identify it. Counted in billed to date with no matching contract value, it would leave every such job over billed by the surcharge at completion and defer revenue the job never earns back through percent complete. The owner chose recognition as billed over adding it to the contract because it is not agreed up front and its total is not known until each invoice is accepted. It prints on the pay application because that is the document the customer approves; estimates written before fuel prices rose cannot be repriced, and the surcharge is expected to fade as new estimates carry the higher cost.

**Affected**: `tenant_policy` (`fuel_surcharge_treatment` becomes the fuel surcharge item ids and the rate; its description no longer cites D-04). F08 (billed to date excludes surcharge lines; the separate figure). F08.1 (the choice per pay application, the printed line, the two-part tie). F12, F13, F14, F17. ## D-40 · 2026-10-06 · PDF documents are produced with reportlab

**Decision**: The platform produces its PDF documents (report exports from F08 onward, and the pay application in F08.1) with the `reportlab` library, added to the backend dependencies at a pinned version. A PDF is built on the server from the same Decimal figures as the screen and the XLSX export; no figure is recomputed for it.

**Reasoning**: F08 is the first feature to export a PDF, and F08.1's pay application is a customer document with a schedule of values that can run over several pages, which needs tables with repeating headers. `fpdf2` was considered as the lighter alternative. One library for every PDF keeps the documents consistent.

**Affected**: F08, F08.1 and later report features. BLUEPRINT §12 (stack). The backend dependency list.

**Figures per job (pure functions, Decimal, computed on read, nothing stored; `app/domain/billing/` beside `amounts.py`)**
A job's documents are the non-voided, non-deleted `billing` rows whose customer row is aliased to the job (`job_alias`, `qbo_customer`). Credit memos are negative (`signed_amount`).
- **Billed to date**: Σ over the job's invoices, credit memos and sales receipts of (total − sales tax − fuel surcharge lines). A fuel surcharge line is a `billing_line` whose `item_external_id` is in the tenant's fuel surcharge items. (Sales tax: "For the owner", answer 1.)
- **Fuel surcharge billed**: Σ of those lines, signed.
- **Deposit invoiced**: Σ of billed-to-date amounts on the job's deposit documents. A deposit document is an invoice whose document number is `<estimate number>_DEP` for an estimate attached to the job **and** whose lines, other than fuel surcharge lines, are all on a deposit item. A document that meets only one of the two is not a deposit: it still counts in billed to date, and the job says so in one sentence ("For the owner", answer 2).
- **Deposit received**: Σ of payment applications against the deposit documents.
- **Collected to date**: Σ of signed payment applications against the job's documents, plus sales receipts (F05: Payments + Sales receipts). Whole-document money, surcharge and tax included.
- **Open A/R**: Σ of the signed `balance` of the job's documents.
- **Unapplied payments**: Σ `payment.unapplied_amount` on the job's customer rows. Not billed to date and not collected to date (D-02); shown as its own figure with a review sentence until an invoice exists.
- **Remaining to bill**: revised contract − billed to date, for a fixed-price job. Shown as it comes out, negative included; when billed to date exceeds the revised contract the job raises `BILLED_OVER_CONTRACT` (§10: likely an unapproved change order). Not shown for T&M, pool or program jobs, which have no contract (D-24, D-30, D-35).
- **Last billing date**, **last payment date**, **days since last activity** (the later of the two against today in the tenant's time zone; cost dates join in F10).
- **Not on a job**: one set of the same totals for documents and payments whose customer row has no job: a tracked row with no job, an untracked row (D-37), and a parent customer of a construction job (D-35: unassigned until moved). One figure per column, not a list of customers.

A surcharge line is recognised on item id alone, so a discount line can never be one. **One caveat the brief's wording hides**: the repo's only QuickBooks fixture is Intuit's sample company (D-25, `tests/fixtures/qbo_sandbox/`); there is no Rye Beach QuickBooks fixture (`tests/fixtures/rye_beach/` holds the chart, the suggestion rules and the estimate workbooks). 2. **`Item` is fetched and held raw today; nothing is added to the sync or the drift check.** `Item` is in `ENTITIES` and in `NAME_LISTS` (inactive rows are fetched too), so the backfill pages it, the change poll asks CDC for it (all sixteen entities), the nightly check counts it, and the webhook list carries it. 5. **Sales receipts enter once each, through the F05 rows.** A SalesReceipt becomes one `billing` row (kind `sales_receipt`) and one `payment` row (kind `sales_receipt`) carrying one `payment_application` of the full total against that billing row (`payment_from_sales_receipt`). Billed to date reads `billing` rows only, so the receipt is billed once. Collected to date reads `payment_application` rows of both payment kinds against the job's documents, so the receipt's self-application collects it once; the brief's "plus sales receipts" is already inside that sum and is **not** added again from the billing row. Open A/R reads `billing.balance`, 0.00 on a receipt. Unapplied payments reads `payment.unapplied_amount`, always 0.00 on a receipt. The Connections month totals keep Payments (kind `payment` only) and Sales receipts as two columns, so the collected tie-out is Σ applications of both kinds + Σ unapplied = Payments + Sales receipts, which holds because applications + unapplied = total on every payment (S-01 extra 2; a credit-memo application is signed negative by `signed_application`, as the zero-total "link credits to charges" payments need).
