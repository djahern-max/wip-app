### Goal
The owner's first draft on 6115758 (pay application 5, dated 2026-10-07) showed that a pay application cannot yet be sent to a customer or safely entered: the PDF's table runs off the page, a work area already billed in full could be entered at 0.00% and printed a negative "earned this application", the form does not start from what has been billed, a draft cannot be edited or discarded, and the Enter key creates an application. This patch fixes those and nothing else.

### In scope

**1. The PDF fits the page (D-40)**
- The schedule of values, with all its columns, prints inside the page margins: the "#" column and the whole of "Balance to finish" are visible on every row and in the totals row. Claude Code chooses how (landscape, column widths, wrapped headers) and says so in the Plan.
- "Billed ahead by" prints as a label with its amount in the summary's amount column, like the lines above it.

**2. A request never yields a negative amount (D-26)**
- For each listed work area, earned to date on the request is compared with earned on previous applications, whatever its source: earlier issued applications, or, on a job's first application, billed to date per work area (D-45). When earned to date is lower, the request raises `BILLING_NEGATIVE` for that work area in one sentence naming the work area, the percent entered and the amount already earned.
- No application, draft or issued, on screen or in the PDF, shows a negative "earned this application".
- What the draft shows for such a work area is owner question A.

**3. The form starts from what has been billed**
- On a job's first application, each work area's percent is prefilled from its billed to date per work area (Part 1's ties); a work area with nothing billed starts at 0.00. On later applications the prefill is the last issued application's percent, as built.
- How a billed amount that is not a whole percent of the price is prefilled, and what the negative check then compares, is Plan question 2, decided by the owner before the build.

**4. A draft can be edited and discarded**
- Opening a draft's billing request shows the percents, the application date and the surcharge choice the draft holds, not zeros.
- A draft can be discarded (owner question B). Issue and Void are unchanged.

**5. The form is submitted by its button only**
- Pressing Enter in a percent field, the date field or the one-percent field does not create or change an application.

**6. The form's layout (D-22)**
- The billing request's table sits in `.table-wrap` at the left under its heading, with the surcharge choice and the one-percent field below it, at desktop width and at 390 px. No other screen polish (deferred by the owner, 2026-10-07).

**Answers to the six questions**

1. **Why the negative amounts appeared.** `draft_lines` in `app/domain/billing/pay_applications.py` raises `BILLING_NEGATIVE` only when `prev.percent` is set and the requested percent is below it. `previous_for` (`pay_application_service.py`) sets a percent only from an earlier **issued** application; on a job's first application it builds `PreviousIn(None, amount)` from Part 1's tied lines, so the check is skipped and `line_views` computes `this_application = earned_to_date − previous`, which went negative: on the owner's draft 5 (#1 at 100.00, #2 to #4 at 0.00) the lines read (10,830.68), (17,142.92) and (132,820.88). **The one place**: the test in `draft_lines` becomes `earned(area.price, percent) < prev.earned` whenever a `prev` exists, whatever its source (an earlier issued application or the tied lines), and the sentence in `app/domain/jobs/issues.py` names the work area, the percent entered and the amount already earned: "Work area #2 "Erosion Control & Site Prep" was requested at 50.00%, which earns 5,415.34 against 10,830.68 already earned on it; <answer A's tail> (D-26)." Comparing amounts is the same test as comparing percents while the scheduled value is unchanged, and the right one when a later estimate version changed the price (D-26 forbids the negative amount). Two consequences the brief's item 2 needs, proposed with it: (a) the same pure check runs **on read for a draft** (`application_views`, drafts only), so the stored draft 5 on `rye-beach`, saved before this patch with #2 to #4 at 0.00, shows as answer A says and never a negative until it is re-saved; an issued application is read from its frozen lines as today; (b) a draft's exceptions are computed on read as well as at save (today `issues` come back on the `POST` response only, so a reopened draft shows none), which answer A's "the sentence beside it" needs; `line_views` itself does not change.

2. **The prefill when billed to date on a work area is not a whole hundredth of a percent of its price.** Example from the brief: #6 Drainage Structures & Piping, scheduled value 35,233.92, billed to date 10,000.00; the exact percent is 28.38174…%. The stored percent is `NUMERIC(5,2)` with a CHECK (0015), so a prefill has two places. The options, with the cents on that work area (earned to date = scheduled value × percent, half up to the cent, as built):

   | Option | Prefilled percent | Earned to date | Earned on previous applications | Earned this application | Keeps "submitted as prefilled earns 0.00"? |
   |---|---|---|---|---|---|
   | **1. Half up to the hundredth**; previous = billed to date per work area (as built) | 28.38 | 9,999.39 | 10,000.00 | (0.61) → `BILLING_NEGATIVE` as prefilled | No |
   | **2. Up to the next hundredth** (the smallest percent whose earned is not below billed); previous = billed per work area | 28.39 | 10,002.91 | 10,000.00 | 2.91 | No: it bills 2.91 the customer has not been asked for, and no two-place percent earns exactly 10,000.00 |
   | **3. Half up to the hundredth, and on a job's first application "earned on previous applications" = scheduled value × that same percent** (the percent the earlier billing stands for; the residue stays in the summary, whose billed before is the job's whole billed to date, answer A, so the amount due is unchanged to the cent); the negative check compares the request's earned with that figure (28.38 passes, 28.37 raises) | 28.38 | 9,999.39 | 9,999.39 | 0.00 | **Yes** |
   | **4. More decimal places** on the percent | 28.3817 | 9,999.99 | 10,000.00 | (0.01) | No: four places still miss by a cent here; and it is a migration (`NUMERIC(5,2)`, the CHECK), out of scope |
   | **5. A tolerance**: a shortfall below one hundredth of a percent of the price shows as 0.00 | 28.38 | 9,999.39 | 10,000.00 | 0.00 shown | No in substance: the columns no longer add (9,999.39 ≠ 10,000.00 + 0.00); rejected |

   Only option 3 keeps the property without a migration. What it changes: on the first application the line's "earned on previous applications" is the scheduled value at the prefilled percent (9,999.39), not the invoice's 10,000.00; the difference (at most one hundredth of a percent of the price, 3.52 on this work area, never more than a cent on a price under 100.00) sits in the summary, since billed before is the job's whole billed to date. D-36's "on a job's first application, from billed to date per work area" is read as "the percent that billing stands for, to the hundredth"; the job page's billed to date per work area (D-45) is unchanged at 10,000.00. On the second application and after, "previous" comes from the earlier issued application's stored percent, as built, which is the same figure. On every work area of the fixture criteria and of the owner's pass, billed to date is 0.00 or the full price, so the prefill is 0.00 or 100.00 and every cent in the criteria is the same under options 1 to 3. **Recommended: option 3. The owner decides; nothing is built for the prefill before the answer.**

3. **How the PDF is made to fit.** Why it ran off: `application_pdf` builds the schedule with `Table(rows, repeatRows=1)` and no column widths, so reportlab gives each column the width of its widest string; eight columns at 7 pt with the headers on one line each ("Earned on previous applications", "Percent complete to date") are wider than the 7.0-in frame of portrait Letter with 0.75-in margins, and reportlab's default `allowTableBoundsErrors = 1` ignores a width excess, so the table is drawn past the right margin with no error. **The change** (D-40, one library): **landscape Letter** (the board PDF already uses it), margins 0.5 in, a frame of 10.0 in; **explicit column widths**: "#" 0.55 in, "Work area" 3.05 in, the six percent and money columns 1.05 in each, 9.90 in in all; the header cells and the work-area names as `Paragraph`s at 7 pt so they wrap inside their column instead of widening it ("Earned on previous applications" takes two lines); the numbers stay one-line strings (the widest, 465,469.59, is 0.54 in at 7 pt Helvetica, inside 1.05 in less the 12 pt of padding); the summary table's "Billed ahead by" row becomes the label with its amount in the amount column, then "No invoice is due"; "Page n of m" positioned from the document's page size (today from `letter[0]`, the portrait width, so in landscape it would sit mid-page). **The test** builds the first application on the fixture through the API and reads its PDF; separately it builds the same table through a function `schedule_table(view, frame_width)` and (a) calls `wrap(frame_width, 10_000)` and asserts the returned width and the sum of the column widths are not above the frame width; (b) for every row and column asserts `pdfmetrics.stringWidth(text, font, 7) ≤ column width − padding` for the one-line cells (so no digit is clipped inside a cell) and that the wrapped cells are Paragraphs; (c) asserts the uncompressed text holds each row's "#n" label and its "Balance to finish" string and the totals row's 465,469.59; (d) "Page 1 of 1" and the second-page test (90 rows) still finds the header on every page (the page count asserted as observed in landscape). The text alone cannot prove the fit, which is why (a) and (b) measure. The PDF's words, figures and the one-function rule (criterion 18 of F08.1) are unchanged.

4. **Why a reopened draft showed zeros.** `startRequest` in `PayApplications.jsx` fills each row from `schedule[].previous_percent`, which `_list` computes from `previous_for`: the latest earlier **issued** application's percent, else "0.00" (Part 1's tied lines give an amount, not a percent); it never reads the open draft; the date is the browser's today and the surcharge choice is "" (unanswered). So on a first application a reopened draft shows zeros, and on a later one the last issued application's percents, not the draft's. **What the form reads now**: when the job has a draft, the draft itself (`applications[]` where status is `draft`: `application_date`, `surcharge_applies` as yes / no / unanswered, and each listed work area's `percent_complete` from the draft's lines; a listed work area the draft left off takes the prefill), and the button reads "Edit billing request"; with no draft, the prefill (item 3): the API's `previous_percent` becomes the last issued application's percent, else billed to date per work area over its price under the option the owner picks in question 2, else 0.00, and the button reads "New billing request" as today. Saving replaces the draft's lines from the request as built (`draft_application`, number kept); the form sends every listed percent, so changing one changes that line only. A pure `requestForm(data)` in `jobs.js` builds the form state from the response and is tested in `jobs.test.js` (a draft present; no draft).

5. **What discarding a draft does** (for answer B, as proposed): `DELETE /api/jobs/{job_id}/pay-applications/{application_id}` by the roles that may enter a request (`can_enter_billing_request`): refused in one sentence unless the status is `draft`; inside the request transaction the `pay_application_line` rows go first (the FK is `ondelete RESTRICT`), then the `pay_application` row; one audit row `pay_application_discarded` on the job with the draft's fields and line count under `before` and `rows={"pay_application": id}` (the audit log keeps the id after the row is gone). The number returns to the pool: `next_number` reads the job's remaining applications and its `_PMT` invoices, so the next request takes the same n unless an invoice `_PMT<n>` exists. Neither table is append-only (0015) and `draft_application` already deletes a draft's lines when it replaces them, so no trigger, policy or migration changes. The job holds one draft at a time, so "Discard draft" is one button beside "Edit billing request" (busy label "Discarding…"), with no reason asked: a draft is re-entered in a minute and the audit row keeps what it held. **The alternative in B ("a discarded draft keeps its number") exists as built**: `void_application` refuses only an application that is already void, so Void accepts a draft, keeps its row, lines and number, needs a reason and writes `pay_application_voided`. If the owner prefers that rule, item 4 adds no route and no audit event, and the button is "Void draft" (asks the reason).

6. **Why Enter created an application.** The request form is `<form onSubmit={submit}>` with text inputs and a submit button, so the browser's implicit submission fires on Enter in any text or date input: Enter in a percent field, the date field or the one-percent field sent the `POST`. **The change**: the form gains `onKeyDown={blockEnter}`; `blockEnter` in `jobs.js` calls `preventDefault()` when the key is Enter and the target is an `input`, and does nothing for a button or another key, so Space or Enter on the focused "Save draft" button still submits (CLAUDE.md: every control works by keyboard) and the submit control stays the only way in. The test is in `jobs.test.js` with fake events (no DOM renderer in the repo and no new dependency). The Void form has the same shape (Enter in the reason field voids): the brief names the request form only, so it is listed under Discovered for the owner's word; one line adds the handler there too.
