## Why
The sixth design feature. `JobDetail.jsx` (1,033 lines) stacks nine blocks under the shared paint of F09.1 and serves two readers at once: the firm, which attaches estimates, confirms work areas, links QuickBooks rows and assigns invoice lines; and the client, which reads the figures, records change-order agreements, enters billing requests and reads pay applications. D-50 (made here) separates them: a client is never given a set-up task and never sees a set-up control. This feature is the client's page. The firm's set-up blocks move, as they are, to a view of their own that F09.7 designs; nothing the firm can do today is lost.

**Reader**: a client role (`client_pm`, `client_admin`, `viewer`). A firm user sees the same page with one extra line.

## Scope
Layout, style, display words and where the set-up blocks live. **No figure, no API response and no accounting behaviour changes.** Frontend only; nothing in `backend/` but the decisions test. Every figure on the page is the same field, formatted by the same function, as before. The one display rule that touches roles is item 7: the set-up view is drawn for firm roles only; the API's permissions are untouched.

1. **Page head** (`JobDetail.jsx`): the job's name as h1; under it "Estimate 6115758 · Sold 2026-06-12 · In progress · Excavation" (the estimate id, status and sold-on date, division, each as today's fields; "(set when created)" stays on sold-on when it applies); at the right the one primary action, which is whatever the page's primary action is today (Plan question 1).
2. **Where the job stands**: a `.facts` block of six figures from the fields the page already holds: Revised contract (bold), Billed to date (bold), Remaining to bill (bold), Collected to date, Open A/R, Unapproved change orders with its count; EAC in the WIP basis as a seventh fact, not bold, with its note. Under the block, the as-of sentence and the contract sentence that sit under the Contract and Billing headings today, in one `.hint`, with their decision numbers dropped. "None" and "Not computed" in words as today.
3. **Attention** (and Dismissed) directly under the facts, as on the estimate page (F09.2). The sentences are the API's; the `BILLING_UNAPPROVED_CO` sentence names the amount and the work-area numbers as today. The block carries a plain left rule and no status colour.
4. **Work areas**: one table for the kept work areas of the original estimate and of the estimates attached as change orders, instead of two. Columns: #, Name, Price, Billed to date, Left to bill, Kind (as today: suggested / confirmed), Change order (No; Not approved; Approved on date), Action. 5. **Invoices**: the invoices on the job as a table (document number, date, amount, the work areas its lines are tied to), with the `_DEP` invoice as its own first row reading 0.00 when there is none, and a totals row reading billed to date. ## D-50 · 2026-10-27 · One practice operates the platform; a client works only on its jobs
**Decision**: The platform is a tool of the CPA practice that operates it (D-17), used by the practice for its client companies. Setting a company up (the QuickBooks connection, the history start, suggestion rules, the chart of accounts and account mapping, policy keys, burden rates, tracking customers and projects, linking QuickBooks rows to jobs, attaching estimates, confirming work areas, assigning earlier invoice lines) is the firm's work; those screens are written for a reader who knows accounting and are reached only by firm roles. A client is never given a set-up task and never sees a set-up control; where a client's screen depends on set-up (a job's QuickBooks link, the deposit item in use), it shows the result in plain words, never the control. The screens a client works on are the jobs and what belongs to them: the job page, change orders and their approval, billing requests and pay applications, exceptions on the job. Every such screen must make sense to a person who has never seen the platform; a brief for one states who the reader is before anything else, and its acceptance includes a pass in a client role. What differs between companies is configuration held as data per company, never code.
**Reasoning**: The owner is the first user, as a CPA, and the practice is the operator. Rye Beach is the first company; other construction companies may follow under the same practice, and the product may later be offered to other practices (a separate deployment each, D-17). Construction company owners are not the intended operator: the accounting side cannot be made simple without being made wrong, so the simplification goes to the client's screens alone, where it is possible. Today the job page carries both readers' work on one scroll and is confusing to both; separating by reader is what lets each screen be plain.
