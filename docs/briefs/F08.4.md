**Reader** (D-50): the **firm** reads and uses the invoice-lines block, which F09.6 moved to the firm's set-up view of a job; splitting a line is set-up work on an invoice keyed before pay applications were in use. The **client** sees the result only: "Billed to date", "Left to bill" and the new "Percent billed" per work area on the job page, and the first pay application's "earned on previous applications" per line.

### Goal
An invoice keyed before a job used pay applications often bills several work areas on one line ("Start of Job", half the contract). F08.1 ties a line to one work area for its whole amount, so such a line can only stay unassigned, "billed to date per work area" reads 0.00 on work areas that were in fact half billed, and the first pay application then asks for the whole scheduled value again. This patch lets a person split one earlier invoice line across work areas by amount, summing to the line, so that billed to date per work area is right before the first application is issued. Two pilot jobs needed it (the hand workbook for the second went wrong twice).

### What already exists and is not rebuilt (checked 2026-10-30 in the synced repo)
- The billing request already takes one percent for every listed work area and a per-line edit after it (`applyToAll`, `PayApplications.jsx`); the prefill on a first application already reads the percent the tied invoice lines stand for (F08.3).
- The deposit is already netted at the job level inside "less billed to date before this application" (D-02, D-36); a `_DEP` invoice not on the deposit item already raises `DEPOSIT_NOT_IDENTIFIED` (F08). Nothing in this patch touches either.
- Per-line `ROUND_HALF_UP` is already the rule (D-36; CLAUDE.md). This patch only says so in OPERATIONS where a reader will look (Docs).

### In scope

**1. A split assignment (D-52)**
- `billing_line_work_area` gains `amount` (`NUMERIC(14,2) NOT NULL`, signed as the line is: a credit-memo line negative). Migration 0018, reversible: existing rows take their line's full amount in the upgrade; the downgrade refuses while any line has more than one current row (one plain sentence naming the count) and drops the column otherwise. Tenant-scoped as today; RLS unchanged; the RLS enumeration test unchanged.
- A line has one or more current rows. The current rows' amounts sum to the line's amount to the cent, or the request is refused in words and nothing is written. One row per work area per line; a work area may appear once in a split. 0.00 is refused.
- A split names its work areas by id (no name reaches a write path). The same rule as today decides which work areas may be picked (kept, on the job's attached estimates); an unapproved change-order work area may be picked and `BILLING_UNAPPROVED_CO` reads the assigned amount (D-45: a flag, no figure changes).
- History: a new assignment or split ends the line's current rows and writes new ones (the F08.1 Plan answer 2 mechanism, whatever it is, extended to several rows); clearing a line ends them all. **One audit row per line acted on**, whatever the number of parts; the row's detail lists the parts.
- Fuel surcharge lines (D-39) and deposit invoices (D-02) are still not offered.
- "#n" lines and pay-application lines are never split: they are tied by rule (D-26, D-36).

**2. Figures (computed on read; nothing else stored)**
- Billed to date per work area = Σ of the tied lines' tied amounts: a "#n" line and an application line for their whole amount, an assigned line for the amount of each of its rows. A line with no row counts in the job's billed to date and in no work area's, as before. "Not assigned to a work area" is the difference, as before.
- **Percent billed** per work area = billed to date per work area ÷ scheduled value × 100, `ROUND_HALF_UP` to 0.01, read-only; blank where the scheduled value is 0.00 or the work area is an unapproved change order. It is a display figure; the prefill on a first application keeps reading what F08.3 reads.
- The job's billed to date, remaining to bill, the revised contract, EAC and over / (under) billed are unchanged to the cent by any split.

**3. Screens (D-22 as amended by D-47)**
- Set-up view, invoice lines block: an unassigned line that is offered gains "Split" beside "Assign". Split opens a small in-row form: one row per pickable work area with an amount field (blank = not part of the split), the running sum and the line's amount shown, "Record split" as the row's action with its busy label ("Recording…"); refused in one sentence in place of the form when the sum differs. A split line shows "Split: #1 2,750.00; #2 5,415.34; …" in the work-area column and "assigned by <display name>" in the how column; "Clear" ends the whole split.
- Job page, work-areas table (client reader): a "Percent billed" column after "Billed to date", `.num`, two decimals, "%" in the header not the cells. No sideways page scroll at 1280 px and 390 px; one primary action per screen unchanged.
- No new colour, no new status word (D-49).

**4. API**
- `PUT /api/jobs/{job_id}/invoice-lines/{billing_line_id}/work-area` keeps its one-work-area body for one call and accepts a `parts: [{estimate_work_area_id, amount}]` body for a split (money as strings with cents, parsed to `Decimal`). `DELETE` clears every row. `GET …/invoice-lines` returns `parts` per line; `GET /api/jobs/{job_id}` work areas gain `percent_billed` (a string, two decimals, or null). Roles unchanged: `firm_admin`, `firm_staff`, `client_admin` assign; every role reads; the role matrix test extended for the new body.

1. **How F08.1 keeps history, and the extension.** One appended row per event in `billing_line_work_area`, never edited (`assigned` or `cleared`; the table is append-only, D-13); the latest row for a line is its state. Extended: an **event** is the set of rows one request writes; they share `recorded_at` (Postgres `now()` is the transaction's start time, and a request is one transaction) and `recorded_by`. The **current rows** of a line are the rows of its latest event, the rows carrying the line's greatest `recorded_at`. A single assignment is an event of one `assigned` row, a split an event of two or more, a clear an event of one `cleared` row; a new event ends the previous one by being later. `latest_assignments` (`board.py`) returns the latest event's `assigned` rows per line (a `cleared` event is none). **Uniqueness**: a unique index `uq_billing_line_work_area_event` on `(tenant_id, billing_line_id, recorded_at, estimate_work_area_id)` where `action = 'assigned'`, so one row per work area within an event and therefore one current row per line and work area; the service refuses a duplicate in words before the index would. No column beyond `amount`; nothing is updated or deleted; `APPEND_ONLY_TABLES` and the RLS enumeration test are unchanged.
2. **The backfill.** The column is added nullable, filled, then set `NOT NULL`. Per tenant with tenant context (`set_config('app.tenant_id', …, true)` as 0012 does; RLS stays forced), one statement: `UPDATE billing_line_work_area w SET amount = CASE WHEN b.kind = 'credit_memo' THEN -l.amount ELSE l.amount END FROM billing_line l JOIN billing b ON b.id = l.billing_id WHERE l.id = w.billing_line_id AND w.tenant_id = :t`; the sign is the document's, as `tie_line` reads it. Every existing row, `cleared` rows included, takes its line's amount (the brief's words), and a new `cleared` row does the same, so the column means "the line's signed amount at the event". **A row whose line no longer exists cannot exist**: `billing_line_id` references `billing_line` with `ON DELETE RESTRICT`, so a referenced line is never deleted; a voided document keeps its line rows with the amounts the void left (0.00 where QuickBooks zeroed them) and its rows take that amount, which no figure reads (a voided document is not counted). The migration reads `billing_line` and `billing` and writes only the new column; it never touches `billing_line`. The downgrade counts, per tenant, the lines whose latest event has more than one row and refuses with "n line(s) are split across work areas; the amount column cannot be dropped while a split exists" (the table is append-only, so the operator ends the splits through the app first), else drops the index and the column. Noted under Discovered, not fixed here: `_write_billing` (`sync.py`) rewrites a changed document's lines by delete and insert, which the RESTRICT would refuse for a line with an assignment row; the F08.1 fixture never re-applied an assigned document.
3. **Where the sum is.** `job_work_areas` in `app/domain/billing/work_areas.py`: `billed_lines[t.area.key] += t.amount` per tied line. The change: `LineTie` gains `parts: tuple[LinePart, ...]` (`LinePart(area, amount)`); `tie_line` builds one part for a "#n" line and for an application line (the whole line amount; `resolve_hash` and the application slot are untouched) and one part per current row for an assigned line (the row's `amount`); the sum becomes `for p in t.parts: billed_lines[p.area.key] += p.amount`, "the row's amount, not the line's amount". `t.area` stays the one tied work area for a "#n", application or one-row assigned line and is None for a split (`how` is `assigned` for both); `billed`, `billed_lines` (what `previous_for` reads for the first application's prefill and "earned on previous applications"), `not_assigned` (billed to date less every part), `unapproved_billed` and `left_to_bill` keep their shape, so the application, the flag and the complement follow without a change of their own. New and pure: `JobWorkAreas.percent_billed(area)`: billed ÷ price × 100, `ROUND_HALF_UP` to 0.01; None where the price is 0.00, the work area is omitted, or it is a change order without an applying approval.
4. **The audit row.** One `audit_log` row per line acted on, `entity_type` `job` as today. Action: `billing_line_split` (new `TenantEvent`) when two or more parts are written; `billing_line_assigned` / `billing_line_reassigned` for one part as today (`before.work_area` keeps F08.1's shape); `billing_line_cleared` for a clear. `detail.before` and `detail.after` each carry `parts`, a list of `{"work_area": "#1 Mobilization", "amount": "2750.00"}` strings (the label as the pick-list prints it, the estimate number added off the original; the amount signed, as a string), with `document`, `line_no`, `line_amount` and `note` beside them; `rows` lists every `billing_line_work_area` id written. `detail.summary` is the sentence the audit page prints: "Line 1 of EST6115758_PMT1 (83,147.24) split across 4 work areas: #1 Mobilization 2,750.00; #2 Erosion Control & Site Prep 5,415.34; #3 Demo Existing Wall 8,571.46; #4 New Retaining Wall 66,410.44."; a replacing split says "split again across n work areas"; a clear of a split says "no longer split across n work areas". The audit page is the JSON read of `GET /api/audit` (F02; no viewer screen exists), so the sentence travels in the row and prints as it is.
5. **The form at 390 px.** A second table row directly under the line's row, inside the same `.table-wrap`: its first cell is empty and held like every first cell (sticky; 9.5 rem on a phone), its second cell spans the remaining columns. In it, a stacked list (`.split-form`): one `<label>` per pickable work area ("#1 Mobilization, 5,500.00") with an amount field (`inputmode="decimal"`, blank = not part of the split); under the list one line, "Sum of the parts 83,147.24; the line is 83,147.24" (or "…; short by 0.01" / "…; over by 0.01"); then "Record split" (`.button`, busy "Recording…", disabled while the sum differs, a part is 0.00 or nothing is entered) and "Cancel" (`.link-button`). The list is as wide as its widest label, capped at 24 rem, so at 390 px the form is a narrow column that scrolls sideways with the other columns under the held first cell, exactly as the table already does; the page never scrolls sideways because the box has `overflow: auto`. A server refusal replaces the sum line with the sentence and leaves the form open. The screen's one primary action (the header form's "Save") is unchanged; the CSS is a few declarations on `.split-form`, no colour.

### Where the build differs from the brief, and what it adds

Scratch and test databases only.
- **The audit detail** carries `parts` on every assignment event (one entry for a one-area assignment), `document`, `line_no`, `line_amount` and `summary` beside F08.1's `work_area`; the F08.1 test's `before == {"work_area": None}` was widened to `before.work_area is None and before.parts == []`. `rows` lists the row ids written (a list, not one id). A new `TenantEvent`, `billing_line_split`, names an event of two or more parts; one part stays `billing_line_assigned` / `billing_line_reassigned`.
- **A split's parts are ordered as the pick-list is** (the original's work areas by number, then the estimates attached as change orders by number) in the figures, the label, the audit row and the sentence, whatever order the request or the database read gives them.
- **Refusal sentences keep the document number's case**: `str.capitalize()` lowercased "EST6115758_PMT2" in the F08.1 refusals ("Line 1 of est6115758_pmt2 …"); a first-letter-only helper replaces it in `assignments.py`.
- **A one-part `parts` body** is accepted: for the line's whole amount it is a plain assignment (`billing_line_assigned`); for any other amount it is refused with the sum sentence.
- **The credit memo's sign is the line's**: a credit-memo line's parts are negative and a positive split of it is refused with the sum sentence naming 1,000.00 and (1,000.00).
- **`LineTie.assignments`** (every current row) sits beside F08.1's `assignment` (the first row: who and when); `area` is None for a split and `parts` carries each work area; "#n" and application lines have one part for the whole line, so `job_work_areas` sums parts for every tie in one loop.
- **The split form is held beside the first column** (`position: sticky; left:` the held cell's width, padding and border plus a gap; 24rem wide on a laptop, 12.5rem at phone width with the labels wrapping), so it stays in view however far the table was scrolled to reach Split, instead of scrolling with the other columns as Plan answer 5 said; the measured renders at 390 px showed the form scrolled out of view otherwise.
- **The job page's firm line** counts a split line as tied (`firmLineWords`: a `work_area_id`, or two or more `parts`).
- **A split with a dropped part** (a work area not on the latest version) unties the whole line with F08.1's sentence naming that part, as a one-area assignment does; the split is one decision and is not half kept.

### D-52 (append word for word)

## D-52 · 2026-10-30 · An earlier invoice line may be split across work areas by amount

**Decision**: An invoice line keyed before pay applications were in use on its job, with no "#n", may be assigned by a person to more than one work area, each part a signed amount, the parts summing to the line to the cent. Each part counts in that work area's billed to date; the line still counts once in the job's billed to date, which remains what the accounting system says (§8.2 column 10). Parts are amounts, never percents; a "#n" line and a pay-application line are never split. One audit row per line, listing the parts. The order of sources stands: pay applications, then "#n" lines, then assignments (D-45).

**Reasoning**: Rye Beach's earlier invoices bill several work areas on one line ("Start of Job", half the contract); two of the first three pilot jobs did. Under D-45 such a line stays unassigned, every work area it billed reads 0.00 and the first pay application asks for the whole scheduled value again, which is the double-billing the application exists to prevent. Amounts, not percents, because the person is reading an invoice and the workbook that produced it; the platform never infers the spread. The job-level figures do not move, so every tie-out in §8.5 is unchanged. Made in express mode at the owner's request of 2026-10-29.

**Affected**: F08.4 (built on it). F08.1 (the assignment mechanism; `billing_line_work_area` gains an amount). F09 (`BILLING_UNAPPROVED_CO` reads assigned amounts). F12 (billed per work area beside cost per work area). D-45 amended as to how many work areas one assigned line may reach; the rest stands. D-26, D-36 unchanged. BLUEPRINT §8.2, §10.
